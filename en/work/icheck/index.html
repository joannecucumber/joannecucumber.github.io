Product Overview產品概覽

iCheck helps people compare beauty-product prices across physical retail channels. It brings together products, prices, articles, and reviews to support shopping decisions.

As the lead product designer, I owned the app’s information architecture, interaction flows, and interface redesign, alongside the responsive website and implementation collaboration.

Business Context & Challenges商業背景與挑戰

The product had accumulated many features. Within a limited timeline, we needed to reorganize its structure and visuals while retaining the existing functionality.

The redesign needed to make products and comparison information easier to find, with reasons to return. Existing data showed higher return rates among people who used search, making search an important entry point to improve.

Strategy & Execution策略與執行

Reorganize the home screen around accessible search

Existing data showed higher return rates among users of search. A more prominent home-screen search entry reinforced the brand’s price-comparison purpose so people could understand that they could find products and check prices. Search supported both products and articles, accommodating different information needs.

The original product arrangement became themed groups such as Editor’s Picks and Buy One, Get One, giving browsing users a starting point. An article feed followed toward the bottom of the page, connecting product and content exploration in one scrolling experience alongside direct search.

Restructure categories and information hierarchy

A review of the existing features and content informed reorganized browsing paths while retaining functionality. Information previously split across product-page tabs became a single scrolling page so descriptions, prices, and reviews could be read in sequence with less switching.

The category page presented beauty care and household necessities through clearer imagery, improving the earlier dark entry screens. Subcategories moved from a female body diagram to product-category icons, organizing choices around purpose and product type without defining the audience through a gendered visual.

Make comparison information visible

Product cards highlighted potential savings, with less crowded text such as the original price. On the product page, details and retailer prices followed a continuous browsing path, with a price-update timestamp to help people judge whether information was still current.

Beyond price, product scores and user reviews appeared alongside related articles and products to support purchase decisions. These appeared along the same scrolling page so checking prices, reading experiences, and exploring related information could follow naturally.

Connect saved products to price-drop notifications

Saving a product enabled price-drop notifications so people could keep track of items they cared about and receive a reminder when the price dropped. This extended a one-off lookup into ongoing tracking, addressing a situation where someone was interested but not yet ready to buy.

A clearer save and price-alert entry near the product information explained how the action helped users follow prices. The design aimed to create a reason to return; its individual effect on notifications or retention was not separately quantified.

Deliver across screens and devices

The redesign covered home, articles, categories, and product pages. Delivery involved prototyping the information architecture and flows, confirming technical constraints with engineering, and reviewing implementation through to launch. Search, browsing, and comparison paths needed to connect while preserving the product’s existing features.

Desktop and mobile website designs arranged the product introduction, core features, and download entry points across screen sizes. The app and website shared a product positioning and visual direction, with text and screen compositions adapted to different viewport widths as part of the overall redesign delivery.

Outcomes & Impact成果與影響

28% → 43%Weekly retention before and after the redesign

The product was featured multiple times in App Store Today and Shopping.

Within the constraint of retaining functionality, the redesign rebuilt the path between search, browsing, comparison, and saving. Retention reflects the overall before-and-after redesign observation and cannot be separated into the effect of each individual design change.
