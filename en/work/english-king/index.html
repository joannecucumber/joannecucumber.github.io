Product Overview產品概覽

Toast English combines vocabulary practice with collecting toast. Users earn ingredients through lessons, unlock toast flavors, and participate in battles and vocabulary teams, adding goals and anticipation to repeated practice.

I worked with PM and engineering to build the product, covering interface design, gamified visuals, product planning, research, and operations.

Business Context & Challenges商業背景與挑戰

CMoney wanted to reach younger users beyond its established finance audience. The team chose vocabulary learning and gamification as a product direction, launched an initial version, and continued iterating from real usage.

The first release needed an understandable, distinctive experience and a sustainable flow of new content. Roughly three months after launch, longer-term retention began to decline. The next challenge was supporting continued learning alongside the initial motivation to start.

Strategy & Execution策略與執行

Product interfaces

The first version connected vocabulary lessons to toast collecting: completing a level earned ingredients and gradually unlocked toast. The interface brought together learning progress, the collection, battles, teams, and rewards so repeated practice had a visible goal. Figma documentation brought together flows, states, and screens for engineering handoff.

The later review release focused on what to practice next. Incorrect answers entered a review library automatically, and users could also add words themselves. Review was the feature that shipped; learning goals, progress analysis, and other 2.0 concepts remained in planning and were not counted as completed outcomes.

Gamified visuals

Visual design covered toast, ingredients, the collection, characters, and rewards, making practice results tangible. Adding flavors also meant supporting different ingredient and collection states, so the artwork followed reusable asset rules to reduce the work of producing each update.

Collaboration with engineering established how images, asset mappings, and animations could be reused. Design owned the visual and asset specifications, while front- and back-end engineers handled animation logic and asset mappings. New flavors could follow the established motion and presentation rules while keeping the collection experience consistent.

Product planning

Product planning covered proposals, feature scope, and the roadmap, dividing larger features into weekly deliverable releases. When retention declined, the proposed priority was review: collecting mistakes, adding words, and practicing again would form the first complete path, while other concepts stayed on the roadmap.

Delivery documentation connected sprint overviews, flows, and screen specifications in the same Figma file, placing interaction logic beside the relevant interfaces for PM and engineering alignment. A QA checklist organized by flow and state gave the team a consistent acceptance scope during frequent releases.

User research

Research combined surveys, interviews, and recurring NPS to understand user profiles, learning-content needs, and the product experience, alongside retention and behavioral analysis. Surveys surfaced demand for content such as example sentences, while interviews revealed difficulty knowing what users had actually remembered after a quiz.

Some users also actively returned to find past mistakes. Together, these signals informed a review-library design centered on learning feedback and a path back to practice. Observed needs remained distinct from assumptions that still required post-launch validation; one piece of feedback could not establish retention impact.

Product operations

Alongside product releases, growth work included content updates and experiments through Dcard, Instagram, filters, and quizzes to reach younger users. The work included content and community assets that translated the learning product’s appeal into entry points relevant to its audience.

Post-publication tracking covered downloads, activity, store feedback, and willingness to recommend, looking at both discovery and use after entry. Channel-driven acquisition and retention around the review release were interpreted separately so operations experiments and product iterations kept distinct observation scopes.
