Laika: Connecting reading and review with AI

An independent language-learning reader, from product definition and interface design to AI-assisted development, turning real articles into material for understanding, saving, and practice.

Product Overview產品概覽

Laika is an AI reader for learning languages through real articles. Users import an article URL, compare the original text with paragraph translations, look up words in context, save vocabulary, and practice through flashcards, matching, and sentence exercises.

I developed this independent project with AI assistance, working across product scope, reading interactions, service integration, and the landing page and marketing assets. The core idea connects reading, lookup, saving, and review, so learning material can accumulate without repeated transfers between a reader, dictionary, and flashcard tool.

Business Context & Challenges商業背景與挑戰

A single unfamiliar word can interrupt an article: leave to consult a dictionary, then find the reading position again. Reviewing it later requires another step, transferring the meaning and example to a flashcard tool. Each task is possible, but the fragmented workflow makes ongoing learning harder.

Laika explores how one person can use AI to build a working product around this problem. Beyond translation, the experience needs to handle article import, waiting for responses, saving learning material, and continuing across devices. Those features need to support the same learning path.

An independent project also requires choices about implementation scope and service costs. I focused first on reading an article and practicing its unfamiliar words, defining separate scopes for a native app and content discovery.

Strategy & Execution策略與執行

Keep lookup inside the reading context

The article remains the focus. Original, translation, and comparison modes let readers choose how much assistance they need. Selecting a word opens its meaning, part of speech, and examples, with deeper AI explanations available. Pronunciation and paragraph audio sit near the relevant content to reduce switching.

Turn saved vocabulary into practice

Saved words and phrases retain their source article, so later review can return to the original context. The web version provides flashcards, matching, and sentence exercises using that saved material, without requiring a separate set of cards.

Classroom notes and sharing extend the workflow into teaching. Notes can be captured within an article and shared with it, keeping the reading material available after a lesson.

Use AI assistance to build a working service

A single-page web app connects article extraction, AI translation and explanations, speech, and Firebase accounts and cloud data. Lookup and explanation caches reduce repeated requests, while saved articles support continued reading.

AI assistance accelerated implementation and expanded what needed checking beyond individual screens. Adding Explore, for example, required updating both navigation and the view rendering that follows cloud synchronization. Missing the latter caused the view to disappear after syncing. That experience brought navigation, sign-in, and post-sync states into the same review process.

Scope the first iOS version around reading and review

The SwiftUI version shares accounts and learning data with the web app. Its first-version scope covers sign-in, the article list, paragraph comparison, saved words and phrases, and flashcards.

URL translation, classroom notes, and Explore remain outside that initial scope. Synchronization updates individual fields to preserve other article and note data from the web app. The iOS version is still in development, with its status distinguished from the web implementation.

Carry the product proposition into its introduction

I also created the landing page, product demo, and Threads feature cards. The introduction begins with learning through real articles, then explains how reading, lookup, saving, and practice connect, keeping the message consistent with the product.

Outcomes & Impact成果與影響

Completed the web reader and product introduction, connecting article import, AI-assisted understanding, saved vocabulary, practice, and cloud synchronization. The work also established the core structure and scope of a native iOS version.

This project extended my design responsibility into whether the product works in practice: choosing what to solve first while addressing data, waiting, and synchronization states. AI helped me implement a broader product independently; scope decisions and verification still required ongoing judgment.

There is not yet sufficient retention, paid conversion, or learning-outcome data. The results here reflect completed product capabilities and workflows. The next questions are whether users finish their first article and return to practice saved vocabulary, before deciding where to invest further.
