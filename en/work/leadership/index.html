Background

  • I have nearly 10 years of UI/UX experience and designed and shipped 20+ fintech and lifestyle apps at CMoney. I moved up through Group Lead roles, and since Apr 2025 I have been head of the design department; the other Team Leads in the department report to me.
  • The department covers product design (UI/UX), visual design, and editorial design, and serves multiple business units.

Problem

  • AI was widely used but shallow: LLM usage across the team was 100%, but in-depth use (information architecture, flows) was only about 5.6%. Without a link to performance, AI stayed an optional tool.
  • Standards weren’t transparent: new hires and the organization disagreed on what “meeting the bar” meant, and gaps often surfaced only at the end of probation. Company values stayed at the slogan level.
  • The organization lagged behind AI: PMs and designers could both produce interfaces quickly, but each used different components and rules, so spec drift and rework grew with them.

My Role

  • Head of Design Department, leading a 40-person design department.
  • Led: the AI adoption program, talent standards, and the org changes.

1 | AI Adoption

What I did

  • Split the design process into three stages and brought in AI stage by stage: pre-design (requirement clarification), mid-design (ideation and visual production), and post-design (quality review). Used an owner model: Group Lead candidates led group evaluations of multiple AI tools and turned them into SOPs.
  • Drove adoption through “adopt → measure → tie to performance → scale”: measured the time saved at each stage against the pre-AI baseline, reset productivity baselines to post-AI data, and built AI into performance reviews and the hiring bar.
  • A deliberate stop: post-design “AI quality review” showed a net-negative benefit, so we closed it and did not roll it out.
  • Set the delivery method by project type: new projects and small features ship as working prototypes (vibe coding); large redesigns stay in Figma.
  • Led the department to automate visual production with AI-agent workflows.
  • Research backing: as first author, published “From Static Profiles to Dynamic Conversations: AI Persona Construction and Human Comparison in Design Research” (HCII 2026, Springer LNCS), which explores using AI personas for investor research and compares them with human participants, as the basis for bringing AI research methods into the team. Paper link

Results

  • Cut workflow time by 60–90%: requirement clarification time down 60–70%, ideation time down 66–90%.
  • Improved workflow efficiency by ~60% on a flagship project I led.
  • Led the department to speed up visual asset production workflows 3–45x.
  • AI success stories emerged across multiple business units.

2 | Capability Upgrade

What I did

  • Review processes grown from early leadership: as a senior designer at CMoney, I ran the weekly Design Review, set a presentation format for designers (first state the design goal and the problem it solves, then walk through a self-check list), led a design team of 3–5, and walked designers through product proposals every week. The design standards and review processes I later built for the department grew out of this experience.
  • Transparent standards: built shared competency standards for product design and visual/graphic design; probation is assessed against leveled behavior descriptions for three competencies (professional, core, and managerial), explained in each new hire’s first week. Company values also come with concrete behavior examples, and I chose to publish the standards openly to build trust.
  • Hiring and development: made AI fluency a must-have and introduced scenario-based interviews; used talent reviews to select Group Lead successors, ran design-decision group training for them, and shifted Group Leads from a “directive” to a “decision-making” leadership style.

Results

  • Gaps with new hires now surface and get addressed earlier in probation, and overall candidate quality has improved.
  • Developed several Group Lead candidates, some of whom have been promoted to Group Lead.

3 | Team Restructuring

What I did

  • Retitled UI/UX designers as Product Designers.
  • Piloted Storybook adoption so PM, design, and engineering share components and guidelines.
  • Trade-off: the bar and short-term load for designers went up, and skill gaps became more visible, so the change is paired with the standards and development programs above.

Results

  • Titles, competency standards, hiring, and training now point in the same direction: designers are expected to make product judgments.
  • Storybook is still a pilot, tracked against the pre-pilot baseline (share of shared components, review rounds, and rework), with no quantified results yet.

Results Overview

  • Cut workflow time by 60–90%; improved workflow efficiency by ~60% on a flagship project I led.
  • Led the department to speed up visual asset production workflows 3–45x. AI success stories emerged across multiple business units.

Reflection

The key to AI adoption isn’t the tools but building it into how the team works: measure first, tie it to performance, and cut losses quickly when it doesn’t pay off.
