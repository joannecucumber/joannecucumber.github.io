Six pieces of work

01 | Toast English (英文知識王): Turning vocabulary drills into a daily habit through collecting and review

Positioning: a gamified English vocabulary app built from 0 to 1. A “toast collection” drives learning motivation, and a review mechanism plus scalable systems lift long-term retention.

My role

  • Lead UI/UX designer, also covering the PO role: roadmap, prioritization, engineering doc templates, and QA checklists.
  • User research: interviews, surveys, and a weekly NPS survey with automated reports.
  • Personally illustrated the toast collection, mascot, avatar frames, and LINE stickers.
  • Shipped weekly at CMoney, with major features every two weeks.

Problem

  • The hardest part of learning vocabulary is sticking with it. Users know English matters, but they don’t feel themselves improving, so the motivation to return fades over time.
  • Post-launch data and interviews showed long-term retention as the main gap: users studied words but couldn’t see their progress.
  • Content had to keep growing (new flavors, new animations). Redoing design and engineering from scratch each time couldn’t keep pace.

Approach (design highlights)

  1. Motivation through collecting + battles: each level maps to a toast flavor, and collecting ingredients unlocks it in the collection. Live battles and “word squads” sustain motivation through social pressure and the urge to collect.
  2. A story framework that makes content scalable: “toast flavors” as the world; ingredients stack and combine, so a full set of flavors can be designed within a week.
  3. 2.0 review feature, so users see their progress: identified the retention problem from data, interviews, and surveys, and built a loop of “start training → see progress → fear of slipping → keep training”; added study plans, weekly goal progress, and weak-word analysis, and strengthened the learning feel of the home screen. I did all of the research and design myself.
  4. Push-permission flow optimization: user research revealed problems in the permission flow, so I added an explainer screen before the iOS system permission dialog, letting users understand what notifications are for and why they matter before the system asks.
  5. Backend-driven animation system: animation assets are now controlled by the backend with a unified motion-path spec, so a large batch of animations was built within two weeks, and every new flavor can reuse it directly.
  6. Research as a system: a weekly NPS survey (shown after users open the app a set number of times, with official stickers as the reward) plus automated reports; LINE stickers as interview incentives keep a steady flow of participants every week.

Results

  • 2.0 review redesign: D1 retention 29.6%→44.0%, D7 retention 24.9%→32.3%, D30 retention 13.8%→17.5%.
  • iOS push opt-in rate 56.8%→64.1% (+7.3 percentage points).
  • Animation system: for each new flavor, design time −75%, engineering time −97%.
  • Next-week return rate (weighted across both platforms) 65%, NPS 54, store rating 4.9★.
  • #1 in iOS Education and #3 in Top Free overall (a team product result; I also served as PO and led marketing planning).

02 | E-invoice app (囂搞發票) + KOL Modular Product Line: Turning one app into a product line that scales horizontally

Positioning: IP- and KOL-partnership apps broken into swappable modules, so every new partnership can launch quickly on the same framework.

My role

  • E-invoice app (囂搞發票) (the first KOL invoice app): proposed and independently designed the features, and built the e-invoice IP module (defining the artist artwork areas and where color palettes switch), the first module in the product line.
  • KOL English learning app: designed it using the English-series modules.
  • Podcast storytelling app: independently planned the feature architecture and pitched it to the KOL, designed the subscription landing page on my own in one day, and also owned the post-launch design.

Problem

  • Every IP or KOL partnership is a new app. Designing each from scratch means long launch times and inconsistent quality.
  • Characters, color schemes, and content formats vary widely across IPs, so the team needed rules for what stays fixed and what can be swapped.

Approach (design highlights)

  1. E-invoice IP module: E-invoice app (囂搞發票) was the first module, with artist artwork areas and color-switch positions clearly defined in the UI; characters, colors, and illustrations are swapped per spec while the core flow stays the same.
  2. English-series module: turned “word bank, lists, and quizzes” into reusable modules; the KOL English learning app applied them directly, swapping only the KOL’s image and content.
  3. KOL content product: the podcast storytelling app’s feature architecture was planned around storytelling; the subscription page highlights “instant savings + a huge story library” to reduce friction on the annual plan.
  4. Pre-launch validation: ran a small usability test before E-invoice app (囂搞發票) launched and fixed the e-invoice carrier linking flow.

Results

  • The IP module built for E-invoice app (囂搞發票) cut the launch cycle of later KOL invoice apps from 3 weeks to 2 weeks, then to about 1 week; the KOL English learning app launched in two weeks.
  • E-invoice app (囂搞發票): store rating 4.9★, named Google Play’s 2022 Most Promising App, and featured on the App Store home page in January 2023.

03 | iCheck Redesign: Making a beauty price-comparison tool easier to navigate and worth coming back to

Positioning: a major redesign of a beauty retail price-comparison app (iOS/Android) and its responsive website, restructuring the information architecture and core flows.

My role

  • Lead designer on the redesign: set the redesign goals and flows, designed before/after versions of the home, article, category, and product pages, and the responsive website.

Problem

  • Redesign the whole app’s structure, information architecture, and visuals in limited time, without removing any existing feature.

Process

  • Usability tests and interviews + online survey → synthesize problems → propose solutions → confirm technical constraints → prototype → engineering acceptance → launch.

Approach (design highlights)

  1. Search first: data showed users who searched returned more often, so the home search bar was enlarged and the home screen reorganized by theme.
  2. Save = price-drop alert: saving a product automatically subscribes users to price-drop alerts, raising push reach and giving users a reason to return.
  3. Flatter information architecture: tabs became a single long-scroll page, articles became a feed, product cards show the amount saved directly, and categories became gender-neutral with icons.

Results

  • After the redesign, weekly retention rose from 28% to 43% (+54%).
  • After the redesign, featured multiple times in the App Store’s “Today” and “Shopping” recommendations, peaking at #3.

04 | ETF Selection Tool: Helping everyday investors quickly find the right ETF

Positioning: a fintech tool that grew out of a personal finance media brand, planned from 0 to 1 and continuously iterated.

I led UI/UX design. v1.0 defined ETF screening and a dollar-cost averaging calculator in a tab structure, wrote screening criteria as plain-language rules, and held rating and push prompts until users finished a main flow. v2.0 planned paid features with a Function Map. v3.0 added broker order integration, so the path became “choose an ETF → calculate → place an order.” v4.0 aligned both platforms and added a watchlist, with the free-tier cap as a natural upgrade point. Finally, a feature audit and visual redesign established a design system (type and color guidelines). Result: near the top of the Finance chart soon after launch.

05 | Small Design System Collection: Making design scale and cutting engineering rework

Positioning: four systems that let the team “build once, use many times,” showing scale and cross-functional efficiency.

My role

  • Designed and drove the four systems below, aligning specs with frontend, backend, and QA.
  • Fanci theming system (2023): I led defining the frontend and backend specs.

Problem

  • When custom requests, content updates, and cross-team handoffs are redone by hand every time, they eat up design and engineering time and invite errors.

Approach (design highlights)

  1. Fanci community color-theming system: designed a theming module for “personalized community experiences,” with a color-token module on the frontend and color codes on the backend; custom themes now only need a new color code, with no app update or store review.
  2. Animation system: unified the motion-path spec and moved animation assets to backend control (Toast English, 英文知識王).
  3. Engineering doc template: requirements overview, logic, and layout in one place, cutting back-and-forth at handoff.
  4. Figma QA checklist: standardized acceptance items and handed QA a complete document.

Results

  • Fanci: each custom theme takes −87% design time and −95% engineering time, with no new store submission.
  • Animation system: each new flavor takes −75% design time and −97% engineering time.

06 | REDPOD Stock-lottery Alarm (personal project)

Positioning: an iOS tool that keeps investors from missing IPO subscription lotteries, launched in December 2021.

  • My role: I did all design and research myself, working with 1 iOS engineer and 1 backend engineer.
  • Problem: surveys and interviews surfaced 4 main pain points: a complicated process, hard-to-find information, unfamiliarity with new listings, and forgetting to apply.
  • Approach: personas and journey maps → competitive analysis → MVP scoping with a function map → a Design Guideline; built around the lottery list, a timeline progress bar, and reminder toggles.
  • Result: a fintech 0→1 project taken from research and MVP to launch on my own.

See also: people-leadership experience such as the weekly Design Review and mentoring designers now opens the design leadership case.
